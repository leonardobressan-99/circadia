About the light

Not a medical device

Circadia is for comfort, ambience and daily routine. A phone screen is far too weak to replace a red light panel or a 10,000 lux light box, and the app does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition.

Three lights, two moments

Light is a signal for your body clock. Bright, white light in the morning says "day"; warm, dim red light in the evening says "wind down".

Deep red≈ 625 nm · eveningThe gentlest light for your body clock and night vision. Best for the last hour before sleep and at night.
Amber≈ 600 nm · eveningA warmer, slightly brighter glow for reading or relaxing, still very low in blue.
Morning sun≈ 5500 K · morningBright, daylight-coloured light to help you feel awake and start your routine.

Why light in the morning helps

Bright light soon after waking is the strongest cue that keeps your body clock in time. It lowers leftover melatonin and boosts alertness, and over days it helps you feel sleepy at a regular time at night.

Best timingWithin the first hour after waking, for 10–30 minutes, with the screen at full brightness and close to your face's eye level (not staring into it).
Real daylight is strongerOutdoors, even on a cloudy day, gives 1,000–10,000+ lux. A phone screen gives a few hundred at most, so a walk outside or a window seat is even better.
Not a light boxClinical bright-light therapy uses about 10,000 lux light boxes. This screen is a gentle wake-up cue, not a treatment for seasonal or sleep disorders.

Red & near-infrared spectrum

Penetration depth & cellular impact

Longer wavelengths travel deeper before they are absorbed. Visible red light mostly stays in the skin, while invisible near-infrared reaches deeper tissue. This is why therapy devices pair 630–660 nm with 810–850 nm.

Red · 630–670 nmAbsorbed in the outer few millimetres. Studied for skin, collagen and wound healing.
Near-infrared · 810–850 nmPasses deeper. Studied for muscles, joints and recovery.
Your phone screen · ≈ 600–630 nmVisible red only, about 100× weaker than a therapy panel and with no near-infrared, so it gives gentle ambient light, not these doses.

Depths are approximate and illustrative; they vary with skin type, power and study method.

Why red is gentler at night

Your eyes have a special light sensor (melanopsin) that tells your body clock it's daytime and holds back melatonin, the sleep hormone. It reacts most to blue light around 480 nm and barely at all to deep red.

MelatoninDeep red affects your body clock far less than white or blue light at the same brightness.
Night visionThe rods in your eyes barely respond to red, so your eyes stay adjusted to the dark. That's why astronomers and pilots use red light.

Screen light vs. therapy devices

Red light therapy (photobiomodulation) uses dedicated LED panels at 630–660 nm and invisible near-infrared at 810–850 nm, with much more power. A screen gives gentle ambient light, not a treatment dose.

How to use it

  1. 1
    Pick the light for the momentSun in the morning, at full screen brightness. Deep red or Amber in the evening, with the screen dimmed.
  2. 2
    Turn off Night Shift and True ToneThey tint the screen and change the colour you chose.
  3. 3
    Place it 0.5–1 m awayPoint it at a wall or the ceiling for an even, indirect glow.
  4. 4
    Set a timerThe light fades out over the last minute, and your session is saved to Stats.

What not to do

  • Don't use it as a treatment or instead of medical care.
  • Don't stare into the screen up close for long periods.
  • Avoid Breathe mode and very low brightness if you're sensitive to flicker or have photosensitive epilepsy.
  • Don't fall asleep with a bright screen beside your face. Use the timer.
  • Don't use Sun mode in the evening: bright white light at night delays sleep.
  • Don't expect it to fix ongoing sleep problems. Talk to a doctor if they last.
